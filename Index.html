The therapeutic relationship

Before anything else, I listen.

The first task isn't to apply a technique. It's to understand. What brings you here? What has been happening? What keeps repeating? What are you beginning to notice?

From there, we can become curious about the layers beneath the presenting problem—trauma, attachment, relationships, adaptations, grief, longing, and the parts of yourself that may not yet have had much room to be heard.

Being in process

There is no map for becoming yourself.

You are the one making the journey. My role is to walk alongside you, help you notice what is difficult to see alone, and stay curious about what emerges.

What brings people in

Sometimes the problem is clear. Sometimes it isn't.

You don't need to arrive with the right words—or even know exactly what you need—to begin.

When life feels stuck

Transitions, relationship difficulties, loneliness, identity questions, difficult emotions, repeating patterns, or a sense that the way you've been living no longer fits.

When the past is still present

Trauma, complex trauma, PTSD, attachment wounds, difficult family histories, experiences that continue to shape your body, relationships, choices, or sense of yourself.

When your inner world asks for attention

Anxiety, depression, shame, disconnection, self-criticism, or parts of yourself that seem to pull in different directions.

When something needs to change

Substance use, addictive behaviors, recovery, grief and loss, or the desire to build a life that feels more intentional and more fully your own.

Core therapeutic processes

Different ways into the work.

My practice is relational and trauma-informed, with particular attention to complex trauma and attachment. The methods below are not boxes to put you in. They are ways of entering the work, chosen according to what is actually happening for you.

EMDR

Trauma processing and integration, including experiences that remain emotionally or physically unresolved.

IFS-informed EMDR

Trauma processing informed by the IFS perspective, with curiosity and compassion toward the parts of experience that emerge.

IFS

Developing a more compassionate relationship with the different parts of yourself and the history they carry.

ACT

Making room for difficult experience while becoming clearer about what matters and how you want to live.

Recovery Management

Practical and relational support for substance use, recovery, and the life that recovery makes possible.

Depth · presence · curiosity

There may be more to the story.

What we experience on the surface is often connected to older patterns, relationships, losses, adaptations, and ways of protecting ourselves. Therapy can create enough safety to look into those deeper places without needing to know in advance what we will find.

Being truly seen can help us develop the capacity to see, feel, and hear our own inner life with greater attunement, compassion, and clarity.

The therapy space

A real room. Two people. Time to slow down.

Therapy happens in an actual room in downtown Wilmington. I want it to feel private, comfortable, and human—a place where there is room for honesty, silence, humor, grief, uncertainty, and whatever else needs to be present.

The relationship itself becomes part of the work.

About Alex

Curious about people, comfortable with complexity.

I was born and raised in Wilmington and have been drawn for much of my life to the questions underneath the obvious ones: how people become who they are, how relationships shape us, what allows us to change, and what it means to live with greater awareness.

My clinical work has been shaped by trauma and attachment, relational psychotherapy, mindfulness, meditation, and years of self-inquiry. I completed my MSW at UNC-Chapel Hill with a certificate in Substance Abuse Studies, trained at Duke, and later worked in residential recovery treatment in Northern New Mexico.

I don't believe I need to have everything figured out in order to sit meaningfully with another person. I'm comfortable with silence, interested in complexity, and willing to explore territory that doesn't come with a map.
